# Calculus II: Comprehensive Reference & Operational Mechanics

## 1. Advanced Integration Techniques

When direct integration or simple $u$-substitution fails, engineers rely on systematic algebraic transformations to evaluate complex area, volume, and force integrals.

### Integration by Parts (IBP)
Derived from the product rule of differentiation, IBP splits an integrand into an algebraically simpler piece and an easily integrable piece.
$$\int u \, dv = uv - \int v \, du$$

*   **The LIATE Rule:** Choose your $u$ term prioritizing items highest on this list:
    1.  **L**ogarithmic functions ($\ln x$)
    2.  **I**nverse Trigonometric functions ($\arctan x$, $\arcsin x$)
    3.  **A**lgebraic functions ($x^2$, $3x$)
    4.  **T**rigonometric functions ($\sin x$, $\cos x$)
    5.  **E**xponential functions ($e^x$)

### Trigonometric Substitution (Trig Sub)
Used to eliminate radical expressions of the form $\sqrt{a^2 \pm x^2}$ or $\sqrt{x^2 - a^2}$ by mapping algebraic equations onto right-triangle geometric identities.

| If the Integrand contains... | Use the Substitution... | Identity Used for Simplification | Geometric Reference Triangle |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\sqrt{a^2 - x^2}$ | $x = a \sin\theta$ <br> $dx = a \cos\theta \, d\theta$ | $a^2 - a^2\sin^2\theta = a^2\cos^2\theta$ | Opposite: $x$ <br> Hypotenuse: $a$ <br> Adjacent: $\sqrt{a^2-x^2}$ |
| $\sqrt{a^2 + x^2}$ | $x = a \tan\theta$ <br> $dx = a \sec^2\theta \, d\theta$ | $a^2 + a^2\tan^2\theta = a^2\sec^2\theta$ | Opposite: $x$ <br> Adjacent: $a$ <br> Hypotenuse: $\sqrt{a^2+x^2}$ |
| $\sqrt{x^2 - a^2}$ | $x = a \sec\theta$ <br> $dx = a \sec\theta\tan\theta \, d\theta$ | $a^2\sec^2\theta - a^2 = a^2\tan^2\theta$ | Hypotenuse: $x$ <br> Adjacent: $a$ <br> Opposite: $\sqrt{x^2-a^2}$ |

### Partial Fraction Decomposition (PFD)
Used to integrate rational functions $\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}$ where the degree of the numerator is less than the denominator. If the degree is equal or greater, **polynomial long division** must be performed first.

*   **Linear Factors:** $\frac{1}{(x-a)(x-b)} = \frac{A}{x-a} + \frac{B}{x-b}$
*   **Repeated Linear Factors:** $\frac{1}{(x-a)^2(x-b)} = \frac{A}{x-a} + \frac{B}{(x-a)^2} + \frac{C}{x-b}$
*   **Irreducible Quadratic Factors:** $\frac{1}{(x-a)(x^2+b)} = \frac{A}{x-a} + \frac{Bx+C}{x^2+b}$

## 2. Geometric Applications (Volumes & Curves)

Mechanical design often requires calculating material volumes, surface areas, or the path lengths of curved components (like cables or continuous-profile cams).

### Volumes of Revolution
Rotating a 2D bounding region around a specified axis creates a symmetric 3D volume.

*   **Disk Method (No Inner Hole):**
    $$V = \pi \int_{a}^{b} [R(x)]^2 \, dx$$
*   **Washer Method (With Inner Hole):**
    $$V = \pi \int_{a}^{b} \left( [R_o(x)]^2 - [R_i(x)]^2 \right) \, dx$$
    *Where $R_o(x)$ is the outer radius and $R_i(x)$ is the inner radius.*
*   **Cylindrical Shell Method:**
    $$V = 2\pi \int_{a}^{b} (\text{Shell Radius}) \cdot (\text{Shell Height}) \, dx = 2\pi \int_{a}^{b} x \cdot f(x) \, dx$$

### Arc Length & Surface Area of Revolution
*   **Arc Length ($L$):** Finds the exact unrolled length of a smooth continuous curve path profile.
    $$L = \int_{a}^{b} \sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^2} \, dx$$
*   **Surface Area ($S$):** Computes the exterior structural skin surface area generated by rotating a curve path around an axis.
    $$S = 2\pi \int_{a}^{b} y \sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^2} \, dx \quad \text{(Rotation about x-axis)}$$

## 3. Engineering Mechanics (Statics & Fluid Applications)

These principles form the direct bridge between raw mathematics and core engineering courses like **Statics, Fluid Mechanics, and Strength of Materials**.

### Mechanical Work done by Variable Forces
Work ($W$) is the integral of a force applied over a distance displacement.
$$Work = \int_{a}^{b} F(x) \, dx$$

*   **Hooke's Law (Spring Compression/Elongation):** Force varies linearly with change in spring length from its equilibrium state ($F = kx$).
    $$W = \int_{x_1}^{x_2} kx \, dx = \frac{1}{2}k(x_2^2 - x_1^2)$$

### Centroids and Centers of Mass
For non-uniform density structures or complex geometries, tracking the balancing point or center of mass is critical.

*   **Total Mass ($m$):** For a 1D thin rod with variable density function $\rho(x)$:
    $$m = \int_{a}^{b} \rho(x) \, dx$$
*   **Moments ($M$):** Measures tendency to rotate around an axis coordinate line.
    $$M_y = \int_{a}^{b} x \cdot \rho(x) \, dx \quad (\text{Moment about y-axis})$$
    $$M_x = \frac{1}{2} \int_{a}^{b} [f(x)]^2 \cdot \rho \, dx \quad (\text{Moment about x-axis, constant density } \rho)$$
*   **Centroid Location $(\bar{x}, \bar{y})$:**
    $$\bar{x} = \frac{M_y}{m} = \frac{\int x \cdot dA}{\text{Total Area}}, \quad \bar{y} = \frac{M_x}{m} = \frac{\frac{1}{2}\int [f(x)]^2 \, dx}{\text{Total Area}}$$

### Hydrostatic Force and Fluid Pressure
Fluid pressure increases linearly with depth ($P = \rho g d$). The total hydrostatic force acting on a submerged vertical surface (like a dam wall or valve hatch) requires integration since pressure changes across the vertical face height.

$$F_{\text{hydrostatic}} = \int_{a}^{b} \text{Pressure}(y) \cdot \text{Width}(y) \, dy = \int_{a}^{b} \rho g \cdot (\text{Depth}) \cdot w(y) \, dy$$
*Where $\rho$ is fluid mass density, $g$ is gravity acceleration ($9.81 \, \text{m/s}^2$), and $w(y)$ is the cross-sectional geometry width profile.*

## 4. Worked Mechanical Calculus II Examples

### Example A: Fluid Hydrostatic Force on a Vertical Valve Hatch
**Problem:** A vertical circular viewing port hatch of radius $R = 1 \, \text{m}$ is installed in a fresh-water tank ($\rho g \approx 9800 \, \text{N/m}^3$). The center of the circle is submerged exactly $4 \, \text{m}$ below the water surface. Find the total hydrostatic force acting on the hatch face.

**Solution:**
1.  Set the origin $(0,0)$ at the center of the circular hatch. The boundary of the circle follows $x^2 + y^2 = 1^2 \implies x = \sqrt{1 - y^2}$.
2.  The width of the circle at any vertical position $y$ spans from $-\sqrt{1-y^2}$ to $+\sqrt{1-y^2}$:
    $$w(y) = 2\sqrt{1 - y^2}$$
3.  The water surface is at a distance of $4 \, \text{m}$ above the origin. Therefore, the depth of water at any vertical level $y$ within the hatch is:
    $$\text{Depth} = 4 - y$$
4.  Set up the integral over the boundary of the hatch ($y$ from $-1$ to $1$):
    $$F = \int_{-1}^{1} \rho g (4 - y) \left(2\sqrt{1 - y^2}\right) \, dy$$
    $$F = 2\rho g \left[ 4\int_{-1}^{1} \sqrt{1 - y^2} \, dy - \int_{-1}^{1} y\sqrt{1 - y^2} \, dy \right]$$
5.  Evaluate pieces using geometric symmetry and property features:
    *   $\int_{-1}^{1} \sqrt{1 - y^2} \, dy$ represents the area of a semi-circle of radius $1 = \frac{\pi(1)^2}{2} = \frac{\pi}{2}$.
    *   $\int_{-1}^{1} y\sqrt{1 - y^2} \, dy = 0$ because the integrand is an **odd function** over symmetric boundaries.
6.  Substitute values back together to solve:
    $$F = 2\rho g \left[ 4 \left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right) - 0 \right] = 4\pi \rho g$$
    $$F = 4\pi (9800) \approx \mathbf{123,150 \, \text{N}}$$

### Example B: Centroid Extraction of a Tapered Bracket
**Problem:** A uniform thin-plate support bracket profile is bounded beneath the curve $y = 4 - x^2$ and above the x-axis ($y = 0$) from $x = 0$ to $x = 2$. Locate its centroid component position $\bar{x}$.

**Solution:**
1.  **Calculate Total Area ($A$):**
    $$A = \int_{0}^{2} (4 - x^2) \, dx = \left[ 4x - \frac{x^3}{3} \right]_{0}^{2} = 8 - \frac{8}{3} = \frac{16}{3}$$
2.  **Calculate Moment about the y-axis ($M_y$):**
    $$M_y = \int_{0}^{2} x \cdot f(x) \, dx = \int_{0}^{2} x(4 - x^2) \, dx = \int_{0}^{2} (4x - x^3) \, dx$$
    $$M_y = \left[ 2x^2 - \frac{x^4}{4} \right]_{0}^{2} = 2(4) - \frac{16}{4} = 8 - 4 = 4$$
3.  **Compute Coordinate position $\bar{x}$:**
    $$\bar{x} = \frac{M_y}{A} = \frac{4}{\frac{16}{3}} = 4 \cdot \frac{3}{16} = \frac{3}{4} = \mathbf{0.75 \, \text{m}}$$
